AI Safety, Prompt Injection & Privacy

Can an assistant distinguish trusted instructions from untrusted content—and protect private data and restricted actions? Examine six fictional Assist AI traces to decide whether a safety boundary held or failed.

YOUR MISSION

Test the boundary, not just the wording

Compare the expected control, an untrusted input, and the observed assistant or application behavior. Identify the primary finding and assess the impact.

All names, accounts, tool calls and AI responses are fictional test fixtures. There are no real secrets, API requests, external actions or stored learner submissions.

01 · SECURITY TESTING METHOD

Trace who can instruct, see and act

An attack attempt is not automatically a defect. A defect appears when the assistant or surrounding application actually crosses a defined security boundary.

01

Define the trusted rule

Identify account ownership, allowed tasks, tool permissions and output-encoding requirements.

02

Identify the lower-trust input

Distinguish the user's request from retrieved documents and tool outputs that may contain hostile instructions.

03

Inspect the observed trace

Use the actual response, action log or rendering behavior; do not infer success from an attack string alone.

04

Write a measurable retest

Specify the blocked disclosure, approval check or safe rendering behavior that would constitute a pass.

Primary finding · roughly 60%

Identify the most important outcome: safe defense, injection, disclosure, unauthorized action or unsafe output handling.

Boundary verdict · roughly 20%

Determine whether the trusted boundary was respected or crossed.

Severity · roughly 20%

Choose an illustrative impact rating. Written evidence and retest steps are not automatically scored.

Learning note: These scenarios draw on common GenAI application risks including prompt injection, sensitive-information disclosure, excessive agency and improper output handling. In real QA, also verify application-level authorization and audit logs: relying on a model's refusal alone is not a security control. Explore the OWASP GenAI risk overview.
