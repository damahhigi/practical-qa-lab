YOUR MISSION

Compare like with like

Review the approved rule, what differs between the prompts or user contexts, and the observed AI output. Decide whether the difference is a defect, justified or not yet supported by sufficient evidence.

All profiles, responses and evaluation numbers are fictional learning fixtures. No live AI calls, private data submissions or localStorage writes occur.

01 · FAIRNESS TESTING METHOD

Measure differences, then examine why

A different answer is not necessarily unfair. Match relevant conditions, inspect observed outcomes, and avoid claiming causation from a single pair or an unbalanced sample.

01

Define the approved result

Identify the factual answer, policy, eligibility rule or accessible route that should apply.

02

Match meaningful variables

Hold intent, entitlement and task difficulty constant when comparing languages or profiles.

03

Assess outcomes and uncertainty

Record accuracy, sample size, access barriers, context differences and possible confounders.

04

Propose a repeatable evaluation

Use paired prompts, balanced datasets, stratified metrics and meaningful acceptance criteria.

Primary finding · roughly 60%

Identify parity, language inconsistency, potential disparity, justified context, accessibility barriers or an evaluation-data problem.

Comparison verdict · roughly 20%

Decide whether outcomes are equivalent, different without justification, contextually justified or limited by evidence.

Severity · roughly 20%

Rate the observed user impact. Written evidence and proposed retests are not automatically scored.

Learning note: These are deterministic, fictional comparisons—not proof of system-wide bias. Responsible evaluation uses consented, privacy-preserving datasets, language review by qualified speakers, matched test sets, confidence intervals and appropriately designed accessibility checks. NIST AI Risk Management Framework provides broader risk-management context.
